# Appendix C — Numerical methods and reproducibility

## C.1 The reproducibility contract

1. **One number, one script.** Every number quoted in the thesis is printed by exactly one script, named at the chapter's *Validation* footer. No number enters the text from a notebook, a memory, or a previous run.
2. **Standalone scripts.** `numpy`/`scipy`/`matplotlib` only; no inter-script imports except the two shared modules below; every script runs from a bare clone via `python run_all.py [--fast|--all]`.
3. **Figures are build artifacts.** `thesis/figures/chNN_figM_*.png` are regenerated by the owning script (`--figures` where separated); the shared style is `scripts/style.mplstyle`. Hand-drawn schematics (concept figures) are flagged `(drawn)` in captions and exempt.
4. **Convergence is demonstrated, not asserted.** Any regulated or truncated quantity is reported with its convergence evidence: regulator scans (heat-kernel $t \to 0$ with Richardson extrapolation), basis-size scans, or lattice-size scans, as appropriate. A quoted error bar is the observed extrapolation residual, never a guess.
5. **Controls run with signals.** Wherever a theorem forces a zero (mirror spectra, diffeo sector, equal-wall quench), the zero is computed alongside the signal in the same run — the Ch. 11/25 discipline, institutionalized.
6. **Caches are committed, small, and disposable.** Heavy outputs land in `v15/data/` (`.npz`/`.json`); every cache is regenerable from its script; figures never read caches that the same run did not (or could not) produce.

## C.2 Shared module: the 1+1D Dirac bag (`bag1d`)

The workhorse of Part II (`ch08_bag_spectrum.py` exports it). The single-particle problem $\psi' = K(E)\psi$ with

$$K = \begin{pmatrix} -m_I & i(E + m_R) \\ i(E - m_R) & m_I\end{pmatrix}$$

is solved by **transfer matrix**: $\psi(L) = e^{KL}\psi(0)$, with $\psi(0)$ fixed to the wall ray $v_0(\theta_0)$ and an eigenvalue declared when $\psi(L)$ is parallel to $v_L(\theta_L)$ — the residual being the projection onto the orthogonal complement, $r(E) = \langle v_L^\perp, e^{KL}v_0\rangle$ (normalized). Root-finding: dense scan for local minima of $|r|$, then bounded scalar minimization to $10^{-13}$; deduplication by relative gap. Complete spectra below $E_{\max}$ are certified by the Weyl-count check (level density vs the asymptotic counting function). Validations (module self-tests, printed by `ch08_bag_spectrum.py`): MIT spectrum vs $\tan(pL) = -p/m$ roots ($3.6\times10^{-8}$); chiral master equation (12.7) at five angle pairs ($3.8\times10^{-7}$ self-test; $7\times10^{-8}$ against the independent solver in `ch12_chiral_bag.py`); the bulk-phase/$(\delta,\delta)$-wall equivalence ($8.7\times10^{-8}$).

**Spectral asymmetry.** $\eta(t) = \sum_k \operatorname{sgn}(E_k)e^{-t|E_k|}$ over *complete* spectra (both branches to the same $|E|$ ceiling — an asymmetric ceiling fakes asymmetry), evaluated on a ladder of $t$ values and Richardson-extrapolated to $t = 0$. Regulator-independence spot-checks (Gaussian, normalized smooth cutoff) per Ch. 9.

**Vacuum charge density** (`ch11_wall_density.py`): $\rho_{\text{vac}}(x) = -\tfrac12\sum_k \operatorname{sgn}(E_k)|\psi_k(x)|^2 e^{-t|E_k|}$, same extrapolation pointwise; the regional integral cross-checks the Polarization Identity value.

## C.3 Shared module: ladder dynamics (`ladder`)

Part III's quantum simulations (`ch20`, `ch21`). Sparse tridiagonal Hamiltonians (up to $5200$ rungs); wavepacket evolution by Krylov `expm_multiply` with step-size control; observables $\langle n\rangle$, $\mathrm{Var}(n)$, $\alpha = \ln\langle n\rangle$, relational exponents by centered differences of $\alpha_a$ against the common time grid (clock-free by construction — any reparametrization cancels in $p_a$). Constraint-closed runs update the coupling $g(\alpha)$ self-consistently each step (240 updates over two e-folds in Ch. 21; halving the update interval moves $\alpha(t)$ by at most $4.8\times10^{-3}$ — half the quoted Friedmann deviation, so the closure is discretization-converged at the level it claims — printed by `ch21_friedmann.py`). Fixed-point launches (Ch. 20) prepare Gaussian packets in $n$ with quasimomentum $\theta = \pm\pi/2$ imprinted as a phase ramp.

## C.4 Block-spectral lattice methods (Ch. 25)

Static metric perturbations depending on one coordinate block-diagonalize over exact transverse momenta: on $63^3$, the Laplace–Beltrami operator splits into $3969$ blocks of size $63$. Spectral (Fourier) derivatives on odd-sized grids (no Nyquist ambiguity); the metric enters through the covariant form $\partial_i(\sqrt g\,g^{ij}\partial_j)/\sqrt g$ discretized symmetrically to preserve self-adjointness. Unit tests gate the pipeline (asserted: the script aborts on failure): flat-space spectrum exact to machine; uniform-shear spectrum to $5\times10^{-9}$ (exactly solvable by coordinate change). Each configuration is diagonalized once and $E(s) = \tfrac12\sum\omega_n e^{-s\omega_n}$ is evaluated at every $s$ from the stored spectrum; the diffeomorphism control runs in every production sweep (contract C.1.5), including the raw $s = 0$ sum that exhibits the covariance window. Quartic fits $\Pi = \Pi_0 + \kappa q^2 + \beta q^4$ over $k = 1..5$ absorb $a_2$ contamination; the fits are over deterministic points, so the quality measure reported with each $\kappa$ is the rms fit residual (the quoted $\kappa/C$ percentages are central-value comparisons, bounded by the diffeo-control systematic of Ch. 25.4).

## C.5 ODE and relaxation methods

Mixmaster (Ch. 24): the BKL-time ODE system with an adaptive high-order integrator; the Hamiltonian constraint evaluated along the trajectory as the quality certificate. Residuals are quoted as *absolute* values of the constraint expression: the initial figure ($7\times10^{-18}$) is pure floating-point rounding of initial data that satisfy the constraint analytically; the drift ($9\times10^{-13}$) is the integrator's accumulated error; any run drifting beyond $10^{-10}$ is discarded by the script itself. Chain relaxation (Ch. 22): gradient descent on $E[\{L_i\}]$ with the short-range core; endpoint identities asserted programmatically (total length conserved to $10^{-6}$; endpoint uniformity; empty-cell endpoint within $10\%$ of the isolated-balance scale $\sqrt2 L_{\text{floor}}$ — the measured ratio $0.956$ reflects the chain's inter-cell forces and is printed by the script). Newton lattice (Ch. 24): FFT Poisson solve on $128^3$; zero-mode handling documented in-script; fits exclude the small-$r$ discreteness region by a stated window.

## C.6 Numbers with provenance

The table of record is generated by `run_all.py --report` into `data/provenance.json`: it runs the registry, captures every script's printed output verbatim, and records script → tier → what it validates → pass/fail status → the printed numbers. It is the object a referee should diff after any code change; the thesis text never outruns that table.
